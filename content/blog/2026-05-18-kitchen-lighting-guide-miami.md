Your kitchen is half done. Cabinets are picked out, countertops are ordered. Then someone asks, "Where do you want the lights?" And you freeze.

This happens all the time. Lighting is one of the most overlooked parts of a kitchen remodel. But it affects how the entire room looks and how well you can actually use it. Get it right and the kitchen feels open, warm, and functional. Get it wrong and you are squinting over the cutting board at 7 PM.

Here is what you need to know before a single wire gets pulled.

## Quick Answer

Good kitchen lighting uses three layers: ambient, task, and accent. Plan all of them before your cabinets go in. Electrical rough-in happens early in a remodel, and changing things after drywall and cabinets are installed gets expensive fast.

## The 3 Layers of Kitchen Lighting

Think of kitchen lighting in layers, not fixtures. Each layer does a different job. You need all three for a kitchen that works well day and night.

### 1. Ambient Lighting (General)

This is your base layer. It fills the room with even, overall light so you can walk in and see everything.

Common ambient fixtures include recessed cans, flush mounts, and central ceiling fixtures. In most kitchens, recessed LED cans are the go-to choice because they sit flush with the ceiling and spread light evenly.

**Planning rule:** one recessed can per 4 to 6 square feet of floor space. A 150-square-foot kitchen needs roughly 25 to 37 cans. That sounds like a lot, but 4-inch LED cans are small and blend into the ceiling.

**Color temperature matters.** For kitchens, stick with 3000K to 4000K. That range gives you warm white to neutral white. Below 3000K looks too yellow. Above 4000K starts looking bluish and clinical. Most homeowners land happiest at 3500K.

### 2. Task Lighting

This is the light that helps you actually cook. It targets specific work zones: countertops, the stove area, the sink, and the island.

Task lighting includes under-cabinet LED strips, pendant lights over an island or peninsula, and recessed cans aimed directly at countertops. Without task lighting, your body casts a shadow over the counter every time you stand at it. Ambient light comes from above and behind you. Task light comes from in front and directly over the work surface.

Under-cabinet lights are the single biggest upgrade most kitchens are missing. They light up the countertop where you actually prep food. Pendant lights over an island give you both task light and a visual anchor for the room.

### 3. Accent Lighting

Accent lighting is what makes a kitchen feel finished and custom. It is not strictly necessary, but it separates a basic remodel from one that looks intentional.

Examples include in-cabinet lighting (inside glass-front cabinets), toe-kick lights along the base of cabinets, above-cabinet LED strips that wash light up toward the ceiling, and decorative pendant fixtures.

Accent lighting adds depth. It makes the room feel layered instead of flat. And toe-kick lights double as nightlights, so you can grab water at 2 AM without blinding yourself.

## Fixture-by-Fixture Guide

### Recessed Cans

Go with 4-inch or 6-inch LED recessed cans. The 4-inch size works well in most kitchens. The 6-inch size fits better in kitchens with high ceilings or larger floor plans.

**Spacing:** place cans 4 to 6 feet apart in a grid pattern. Keep them about 2 feet from the wall so the light washes down the cabinets without creating harsh shadows.

**IC-rated fixtures** are required if the ceiling has insulation above it (most homes do). IC stands for insulation contact. Using a non-IC fixture against insulation is a fire risk. Always specify IC-rated.

**LED vs. traditional:** always LED. They last 25,000+ hours, run cool, and use a fraction of the electricity. The price difference is minimal now. There is no reason to install incandescent or halogen in 2026.

### Pendant Lights Over the Island

Pendants are the statement piece. They hang over the island or peninsula and give you both task light and visual personality.

**Height:** hang them 30 to 36 inches above the countertop. Too low and you will hit your head or block sightlines. Too high and they lose impact and throw light too wide.

**Spacing:** place pendants 24 to 30 inches apart, centered over the island. Use odd numbers. One pendant works for a small island. Three pendants work for a standard 6 to 8-foot island. Two pendants look awkward because the eye wants a center point.

**Size:** the pendant diameter should be about one-third the width of the island. A 36-inch-wide island calls for pendants around 12 inches across.

### Under-Cabinet LED Lighting

This is the workhorse of kitchen task lighting. LED strips or puck lights mount to the underside of your upper cabinets and illuminate the countertop below.

**Hardwired vs. plug-in:** hardwired looks cleaner and is the better choice during a remodel since the walls are already open. Plug-in systems are fine for a quick upgrade on an existing kitchen, but you will see the cord.

**Color temperature:** 3000K warm white is the standard. It matches most ambient lighting and makes food look natural. Avoid cool white (5000K+) because it makes everything look harsh.

**Dimmable is preferred.** You want full brightness for chopping and low brightness for movie night. Many LED strips come with built-in dimming. If yours does not, add a dimmer switch on the circuit.

**Placement:** mount the strips toward the front edge of the cabinet (closest to you) rather than the back. This throws light onto the countertop where your hands are, not onto the backsplash behind the counter.

### Flush and Semi-Flush Mounts

If your kitchen ceiling is 8 feet or lower, recessed cans may not be possible everywhere (especially if there are joists, ductwork, or plumbing in the way). Flush mounts sit directly against the ceiling. Semi-flush mounts drop down 4 to 8 inches.

These work well as a central fixture paired with recessed cans around the perimeter. They also work in breakfast nooks or over a kitchen table.

### Track Lighting

Track lighting is a flexible option, especially for galley kitchens or kitchens where the ceiling will not accommodate recessed cans. A single track can hold multiple adjustable heads that you aim wherever you need light.

The downside: track lighting looks more utilitarian than recessed or pendant options. But modern track systems come in low-profile designs that blend in much better than the bulky tracks from the 1990s.

## Miami-Specific Lighting Considerations

Miami kitchens have a few things working in their favor. Big windows are common, especially in newer construction. That means plenty of natural light during the day. Sliding glass doors to a patio or lanai add even more.

But here is the flip side. When the sun goes down at 7 PM (or 5:30 PM in winter), all that window space becomes dark glass. Your kitchen lighting plan has to carry the full load from evening through morning. A kitchen that looks great at noon can feel like a cave at 8 PM if the artificial lighting was an afterthought.

**Open floor plans** are standard in most Miami homes built in the last 20 years. That means your kitchen lighting bleeds into the living area. If you put 5000K daylight bulbs in the kitchen and 2700K warm bulbs in the living room, the transition looks jarring. Plan your color temperatures to flow between rooms.

**Humidity.** Miami's moisture levels are no joke. For any fixtures near the sink, stove, or exterior walls, make sure they are rated for damp locations. This is especially important for under-cabinet lights near the sink and any recessed cans near range hoods.

## Common Kitchen Lighting Mistakes

### Not Enough Light Over the Sink

People forget the sink. It is where you wash produce, clean dishes, and handle raw meat. A single recessed can directly above the sink is essential. If your sink is under a window, the natural light helps during the day but you still need a fixture for nighttime.

### Forgetting the Pantry

Walk-in pantries need their own light. A small LED fixture with a door-activated switch is the simplest solution. Otherwise you are holding your phone flashlight while looking for the canned tomatoes.

### All Lights on One Switch

This is the biggest mistake. If every light in the kitchen turns on and off from one switch, you have no control. You want zones. The recessed cans on one switch or dimmer. Under-cabinet lights on another. Pendants on a third. This lets you set the mood and save energy.

Dimmer switches cost $75 to $150 installed and are worth every penny. A fully lit kitchen is great for cooking. A dimmed kitchen is great for entertaining.

### Wrong Color Temperature

Mixing color temperatures makes a kitchen look disjointed. A 5000K recessed can next to a 2700K pendant looks off. Pick one temperature range (3000K to 3500K is our recommendation) and use it consistently across all fixtures.

### Pendants Hung Too Low

We see this constantly. Pendants that look perfect in the showroom end up hanging at face level over the island. Measure 30 to 36 inches from the countertop surface, not from the ceiling. And account for anyone in the house over 6 feet tall.

## When to Plan Your Kitchen Lighting

**Before cabinets are installed.** This is the answer. Full stop.

Electrical rough-in (running wires through the walls and ceiling) happens early in a remodel, before drywall goes up. Your electrician needs to know where every switch, outlet, and junction box goes before the walls close up.

## FAQ

### How many recessed lights does a kitchen need?

Plan for one recessed can per 4 to 6 square feet of floor space. A 10x12 kitchen (120 sq ft) needs about 20 to 30 cans. Use 4-inch LED cans for a clean look. Supplement with task and accent lighting so the recessed cans do not carry the entire load.

### What color temperature is best for kitchen lighting?

Stick with 3000K to 3500K for most kitchens. This range gives you a warm, natural white that makes food look appetizing and skin tones look healthy. Avoid going below 2700K (too orange) or above 4000K (too clinical). Keep all fixtures in the same range.

### Are LED under-cabinet lights worth it?

Absolutely. Under-cabinet LEDs are one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost upgrades in a kitchen remodel. They eliminate counter shadows, make prep work safer, and add a finished look. Hardwired versions during a remodel cost $200 to $600 per run and last for years.

### Can I add recessed lights after cabinets are installed?

You can, but it costs more. An electrician has to cut into the finished ceiling, run new wiring, and patch any damage. Expect to pay 50 to 100 percent more per fixture compared to installing during the rough-in phase. Plan lighting before cabinets go in whenever possible.

### Should pendant lights over the island match the rest of the kitchen?

They do not have to match exactly, but they should complement the overall style. Pendants are a chance to add personality. A modern pendant in a transitional kitchen can work well. The key is matching the metal finish and color temperature so the pendants feel intentional, not random.

### How much does a full kitchen lighting upgrade cost in Miami?

A complete lighting package runs $2,000 to $5,000 for a mid-range kitchen. This covers recessed cans, under-cabinet LEDs, a few pendant lights, and dimmer switches. High-end kitchens with designer fixtures and full accent lighting can run $5,000 to $10,000 or more.

## Las 3 Capas de Iluminacion de Cocina

Piensa en la iluminacion de cocina como capas, no como accesorios individuales. Cada capa cumple una funcion diferente. Necesitas las tres para una cocina que funcione bien de dia y de noche.

### 1. Iluminacion Ambiental (General)

Esta es tu capa base. Llena el espacio con luz uniforme para que puedas entrar y ver todo.

Los accesorios ambientales comunes incluyen luces empotradas, plafones y lamparas de techo centrales. En la mayoria de las cocinas, las luces empotradas LED son la opcion preferida porque quedan al ras del techo y distribuyen la luz de manera uniforme.

**Regla de planificacion:** una luz empotrada por cada 4 a 6 pies cuadrados de piso. Una cocina de 150 pies cuadrados necesita aproximadamente 25 a 37 luces. Suena como mucho, pero las luces LED de 4 pulgadas son pequenas y se integran al techo.

**La temperatura de color importa.** Para cocinas, usa entre 3000K y 4000K. Ese rango te da blanco calido a blanco neutro. Por debajo de 3000K se ve demasiado amarillo. Por encima de 4000K empieza a verse azulado y clinico. La mayoria de los propietarios quedan mas contentos con 3500K.

### 2. Iluminacion de Tarea

Esta es la luz que te ayuda a cocinar. Se enfoca en zonas de trabajo especificas: topes, la zona de la estufa, el fregadero y la isla.

La iluminacion de tarea incluye tiras LED debajo de los gabinetes, lamparas colgantes sobre la isla o peninsula, y luces empotradas apuntadas directamente a los topes. Sin iluminacion de tarea, tu cuerpo proyecta sombra sobre el tope cada vez que te paras frente a el. La luz ambiental viene de arriba y detras de ti. La luz de tarea viene de frente y directamente sobre la superficie de trabajo.

Las luces debajo de los gabinetes son la mejora individual mas importante que la mayoria de las cocinas no tienen. Iluminan el tope donde realmente preparas la comida. Las lamparas colgantes sobre la isla te dan luz de tarea y un punto visual para el espacio.

### 3. Iluminacion de Acento

La iluminacion de acento es lo que hace que una cocina se sienta terminada y personalizada. No es estrictamente necesaria, pero separa una remodelacion basica de una que se ve intencional.

Los ejemplos incluyen iluminacion dentro de gabinetes (gabinetes con puertas de vidrio), luces de zocalo a lo largo de la base de los gabinetes, tiras LED encima de los gabinetes que proyectan luz hacia el techo, y lamparas colgantes decorativas.

La iluminacion de acento agrega profundidad. Hace que el espacio se sienta en capas en lugar de plano. Y las luces de zocalo funcionan como luces nocturnas, para que puedas buscar agua a las 2 AM sin encandilarte.

## Guia Accesorio por Accesorio

### Luces Empotradas

Usa luces empotradas LED de 4 o 6 pulgadas. El tamano de 4 pulgadas funciona bien en la mayoria de las cocinas. El de 6 pulgadas es mejor para cocinas con techos altos o espacios mas grandes.

**Espaciado:** coloca las luces de 4 a 6 pies de distancia en un patron de cuadricula. Mantenlas a unos 2 pies de la pared para que la luz bane los gabinetes sin crear sombras duras.

**Accesorios con clasificacion IC** son necesarios si el techo tiene aislamiento encima (la mayoria de los hogares lo tienen). IC significa contacto con aislamiento. Usar un accesorio sin clasificacion IC contra el aislamiento es un riesgo de incendio. Siempre especifica clasificacion IC.

**LED vs. tradicional:** siempre LED. Duran 25,000+ horas, no generan calor excesivo y usan una fraccion de la electricidad. La diferencia de precio es minima ahora. No hay razon para instalar incandescentes o halogenas en 2026.

### Lamparas Colgantes Sobre la Isla

Las colgantes son la pieza de declaracion. Se cuelgan sobre la isla o peninsula y te dan tanto luz de tarea como personalidad visual.

**Altura:** cualgalas de 30 a 36 pulgadas por encima del tope. Muy bajas y te golpearas la cabeza o bloquearas las lineas de vision. Muy altas y pierden impacto y la luz se dispersa demasiado.

**Espaciado:** coloca las colgantes de 24 a 30 pulgadas de distancia, centradas sobre la isla. Usa numeros impares. Una colgante funciona para una isla pequena. Tres colgantes funcionan para una isla estandar de 6 a 8 pies. Dos colgantes se ven extranas porque el ojo busca un punto central.

**Tamano:** el diametro de la colgante debe ser aproximadamente un tercio del ancho de la isla. Una isla de 36 pulgadas de ancho necesita colgantes de unas 12 pulgadas de diametro.

### Iluminacion LED Debajo de los Gabinetes

Este es el caballo de batalla de la iluminacion de tarea en la cocina. Las tiras LED o luces de disco se montan en la parte inferior de los gabinetes superiores e iluminan el tope debajo.

**Cableado vs. enchufable:** el cableado se ve mas limpio y es la mejor opcion durante una remodelacion porque las paredes ya estan abiertas. Los sistemas enchufables estan bien para una mejora rapida en una cocina existente, pero vas a ver el cable.

**Temperatura de color:** 3000K blanco calido es el estandar. Combina con la mayoria de la iluminacion ambiental y hace que la comida se vea natural. Evita el blanco frio (5000K+) porque hace que todo se vea aspero.

**Se prefiere regulable.** Quieres brillo completo para picar y brillo bajo para la noche de peliculas. Muchas tiras LED vienen con regulacion integrada. Si las tuyas no la tienen, agrega un regulador en el circuito.

**Ubicacion:** monta las tiras hacia el borde frontal del gabinete (mas cerca de ti) en lugar de la parte trasera. Esto proyecta luz sobre el tope donde estan tus manos, no sobre el protector de salpicaduras detras del tope.

### Plafones y Semi-Plafones

Si el techo de tu cocina tiene 8 pies o menos, las luces empotradas pueden no ser posibles en todas partes (especialmente si hay vigas, ductos o plomeria en el camino). Los plafones se colocan directamente contra el techo. Los semi-plafones cuelgan de 4 a 8 pulgadas.

Funcionan bien como accesorio central combinados con luces empotradas alrededor del perimetro. Tambien funcionan en desayunadores o sobre una mesa de cocina.

### Iluminacion de Riel

La iluminacion de riel es una opcion flexible, especialmente para cocinas tipo galera o cocinas donde el techo no permite luces empotradas. Un solo riel puede sostener multiples cabezales ajustables que apuntas donde necesites luz.

La desventaja: la iluminacion de riel se ve mas utilitaria que las empotradas o colgantes. Pero los sistemas modernos vienen en disenos de perfil bajo que se integran mucho mejor que los rieles voluminosos de los 1990s.

## Errores Comunes de Iluminacion de Cocina

### Poca Luz Sobre el Fregadero

La gente olvida el fregadero. Es donde lavas los vegetales, limpias los platos y manejas la carne cruda. Una luz empotrada directamente sobre el fregadero es esencial. Si tu fregadero esta debajo de una ventana, la luz natural ayuda durante el dia pero aun necesitas un accesorio para la noche.

### Olvidar la Despensa

Las despensas tipo walk-in necesitan su propia luz. Un pequeno accesorio LED con un interruptor activado por la puerta es la solucion mas simple. De lo contrario vas a estar sosteniendo la linterna del telefono mientras buscas los tomates enlatados.

### Todas las Luces en un Solo Interruptor

Este es el error mas grande. Si cada luz en la cocina se enciende y apaga desde un solo interruptor, no tienes control. Quieres zonas. Las empotradas en un interruptor o regulador. Las luces debajo de gabinetes en otro. Las colgantes en un tercero. Esto te permite ajustar el ambiente y ahorrar energia.

Los reguladores cuestan $75 a $150 instalados y valen cada centavo. Una cocina completamente iluminada es genial para cocinar. Una cocina con luz tenue es genial para entretener.

### Temperatura de Color Incorrecta

Mezclar temperaturas de color hace que una cocina se vea desarticulada. Una luz empotrada de 5000K al lado de una colgante de 2700K se ve mal. Elige un rango de temperatura (3000K a 3500K es nuestra recomendacion) y usalo consistentemente en todos los accesorios.

### Colgantes Demasiado Bajas

Vemos esto constantemente. Las colgantes que se ven perfectas en la tienda terminan colgando a la altura de la cara sobre la isla. Mide de 30 a 36 pulgadas desde la superficie del tope, no desde el techo. Y toma en cuenta a cualquier persona en la casa que mida mas de 6 pies.

Planifica una luz empotrada por cada 4 a 6 pies cuadrados de piso. Una cocina de 10x12 (120 pies cuadrados) necesita aproximadamente 20 a 30 luces. Usa luces LED de 4 pulgadas para un aspecto limpio. Complementa con iluminacion de tarea y acento para que las empotradas no lleven toda la carga.

### Cual es la mejor temperatura de color para la iluminacion de cocina?

Usa entre 3000K y 3500K para la mayoria de las cocinas. Este rango te da un blanco calido y natural que hace que la comida se vea apetitosa y los tonos de piel se vean saludables. Evita bajar de 2700K (muy anaranjado) o subir de 4000K (muy clinico). Manten todos los accesorios en el mismo rango.

Absolutamente. Los LEDs debajo de gabinetes son una de las mejoras de mayor impacto y menor costo en una remodelacion de cocina. ### Cuanto cuesta una actualizacion completa de iluminacion de cocina en Miami?

Un paquete completo de iluminacion cuesta entre $2,000 y $5,000 para una cocina de rango medio. Esto cubre luces empotradas, LEDs debajo de gabinetes, algunas lamparas colgantes y reguladores de intensidad. Las cocinas de alta gama con accesorios de disenador e iluminacion de acento completa pueden costar de $5,000 a $10,000 o mas.
