## The Numbers Online Do Not Agree With Each Other

Search this topic and you will find a Miami average around $1,491 to $2,278, with a low end near $311. You will also find a national average near $3,000, a range of $1,500 to $15,000, and South Florida tile builds quoted at $12,000 to $25,000.

That is a seventy-fold spread on the same three words. It is not because anyone is lying. It is because those numbers describe different scopes, and almost none of them say which.

Our published range for a tiled tub-to-shower conversion in Miami-Dade is **$4,500 to $12,000**. Below is what actually sits inside that number, item by item, so you can check any quote against it.

![Finished tiled walk-in shower seen from the bathroom doorway with a notepad on the vanity](/images/blog/tub-to-shower-conversion-cost-miami-line-by-line.jpg "Every line below ends up inside a room that looks like this. The cheap quotes are usually missing three or four of them.")

## The Line Items

These are working ranges for a standard alcove conversion in Miami-Dade. Your bathroom will not hit the middle of every line.

**Protection, demolition, tub removal, and haul-away: $450 to $1,200.**
Floor and hallway protection, removing the tub and surround, and getting the debris off your property. A cast iron tub costs more here than an acrylic one because it has to be cut apart to get it out of the room.

**Plumbing adjustment: $350 to $2,400.**
A tub drain is 1.5 inches and a shower drain is 2 inches, so some plumbing work happens on every conversion. Keeping the drain near its current location sits at the bottom of this range. Relocating it in a slab home, which means opening concrete, changing the line, and patching, drives it to the top.

**Framing and substrate repair: $0 to $900.**
Zero if the wall is sound. This is the line that appears after demolition, not before, which is why your scope needs to say in writing what happens if damage is found.

**Cement backer board: $250 to $700.**
The substrate the waterproofing goes onto. Backer board is not a water barrier by itself, and anyone who tells you it is has told you something important about their work.

**Waterproofing membrane: $350 to $1,100.**
The layer that decides whether this shower lasts. Applied over the board and worked into every corner, seam, and change of plane.

![Waterproofing membrane applied in a shower before tile](/images/glenvar-waterproofing.jpeg "This is the line item you will never see again after tile day, and the first one a cheap quote deletes.")

**Shower pan construction: $450 to $1,400.**
Built and sloped on site to drain correctly. A prefabricated base costs less and is a legitimate choice, but it fixes your size and your curb height.

**Tile material: $350 to $2,500.**
Standard porcelain at the bottom. Large format, mosaics, trim pieces, and imported material at the top. This is the line you control most directly.

**Tile labor: $1,100 to $3,200.**
Driven by layout complexity more than by square footage. A straight-set field tile is fast. A herringbone pattern, a mitered outside corner, a niche with trim, and a bench all add hours.

**Grout, trim, and sealing: $180 to $600.**

**Fixtures: $220 to $1,200.**
Valve trim, shower head, handheld on a slide bar, grab bars. Grab bars need blocking installed behind the wall on day two, so decide about them early even if you install them later.

**Glass: $550 to $3,500.**
A fixed panel is the low end. A frameless custom enclosure with a door is the high end. Glass is templated after tile is finished, then fabricated, which is commonly one to two weeks.

**Permit and inspections: $200 to $600.**
A conversion touches plumbing, so it needs a permit in Miami-Dade even under HB 803. The full explanation is in [do you need a permit to convert a tub to a shower](/en/blog/tub-to-shower-permit-miami-dade).

**Final cleanup and water test: $150 to $400.**

## Why the Columns Do Not Simply Add Up

Add the low column and you land near $4,600, which is why our published floor is $4,500.

Do not add the high column. It comes to nearly $20,000, and no real project hits the top of every line at once. A homeowner who relocates the drain through a slab is usually not also buying imported mosaic and a frameless enclosure. The high end of a real Miami conversion lands around $12,000 because expensive choices cluster in two or three lines, not thirteen.

That is the honest way to read any itemized estimate. Look at which lines are at the top of their range and ask whether those are the lines you care about.

## What the Cheap Numbers Actually Cover

A $311 tub-to-shower conversion is not a shower. Neither is $1,491. Once you have read the list above, it is obvious why. Those figures come from cost aggregators that average a wide mix of jobs, including partial labor tasks. They typically describe one or two of these:

- Removing a tub with no replacement work included
- Installing a prefabricated insert into an alcove that is already prepared
- A refinishing or reglazing job on an existing tub
- A labor-only quote where the homeowner supplies every material
- A tub cut, which is a section removed from the tub wall for easier entry, not a conversion

None of those include a pan, waterproofing, tile, glass, permits, or disposal. If a quote you receive is anywhere near those numbers, do not assume you found a deal. Assume the scope is different, and go find out which lines are missing.

## Three Tiers of the Same Project

The most useful thing you can ask for is three prices for the same scope. Here is roughly how they separate:

**Standard, about $4,500 to $6,500.** Same footprint. Drain stays put. Standard porcelain, straight set. Fixed glass panel. One niche. Sound walls.

**Upgraded, about $6,500 to $9,000.** Same footprint. Large format tile or a modest accent. Bench or a larger niche. Better fixtures. Frameless panel with a door. Some framing repair budgeted.

**Custom, about $9,000 to $12,000.** Widened opening or low curb. Linear drain. Full-height tile with pattern work. Frameless enclosure. Handheld, grab bars, and integrated storage.

## The Cost Drivers Worth Knowing Before You Choose Anything

**The drain is the biggest single variable.** Keeping it near where it is can be the difference between the standard tier and the custom tier all by itself, because most Miami homes sit on concrete slabs. Ask about this on the first visit.

**Tile layout costs more than tile price.** People budget for the tile and forget the labor multiplier that comes with a pattern. If the budget is tight, buy nicer tile and set it straight rather than cheaper tile in a complicated layout.

**Glass is the easiest line to move.** Going from a frameless enclosure to a fixed panel can save a four-figure sum and often works better in a small bathroom anyway.

**Hidden damage is real and it is not a sales tactic.** In homes built before 1990 we find water damage behind the old surround often enough that we budget for it. What matters is that your scope says what happens when it appears.

## How to Compare Two Estimates Properly

Do not compare the totals. Line them up and check for these thirteen items. Then ask three questions:

1. **Which items are allowances rather than selected materials?** An allowance is a placeholder. It changes when you pick the actual tile or glass.
2. **What is excluded?** Disposal, permits, and glass are the three most commonly left off.
3. **What is the written process if there is damage behind the wall?**

An estimate that answers those three is worth more than an estimate that is $800 cheaper and answers none of them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is your low end $4,500 when websites list Miami averages under $2,300?**

Because those averages are not describing a finished tiled shower. Cost aggregators pool a wide mix of jobs under one label, including tub removals with no replacement, prefabricated insert installations into prepared alcoves, tub reglazing, tub cuts, and labor-only quotes where the homeowner buys all the material. Average those together and you get a number in the low thousands that does not correspond to any complete project. A finished tiled conversion has to include demolition and haul-away, plumbing adjustment for the drain size change, backer board, a waterproofing membrane, a sloped pan, tile and tile labor, grout, fixtures, glass, a permit, and a final water test. Add the realistic floor of each of those and you land near $4,600. That is arithmetic, not positioning.

**What is the single fastest way to lower the price without regretting it?**

Keep the drain where it is and simplify the glass. Those two decisions move more money than any tile choice, and neither one shows up as a compromise in daily use. Drain relocation in a slab home means opening concrete, rerouting the line, patching, and rebuilding the floor, which can add well over a thousand dollars on its own. Custom frameless enclosures are the other large line, and in a small Miami bathroom a single fixed panel often performs better anyway because it leaves the room feeling open and gives you less hardware to clean. What we would not cut is waterproofing, the pan build, or the permit. Those are the three items where saving money now reliably costs more later, and all three are invisible once the tile is on.

**Does a tub-to-shower conversion have a cost per square foot I can use?**

Not usefully, and be careful with anyone who quotes one. A conversion is dominated by fixed costs that do not scale with area. Demolition, drain work, the pan build, the permit, and the glass cost roughly the same in a 30 square foot alcove as they do in a 40 square foot one. Only tile material and part of the tile labor track square footage. That means a cost-per-square-foot figure derived from a large custom shower will badly overprice a standard alcove, and one derived from a simple job will badly underprice anything with a bench, a curbless entry, or a relocated drain. Ask for the itemized breakdown instead. It takes two extra minutes to read and it tells you something a single multiplier never can.

## Las Líneas del Presupuesto

**Protección, demolición, retiro de la tina y escombros: $450 a $1,200.**
Protección de pisos y pasillo, sacar la tina y el revestimiento, y llevarse los escombros. Una tina de hierro fundido cuesta más aquí que una de acrilico porque hay que cortarla para sacarla del cuarto.

**Ajuste de plomería: $350 a $2,400.**
Un desagüe de tina es de 1.5 pulgadas y uno de ducha es de 2 pulgadas, así que siempre hay trabajo de plomería. Dejar el desagüe cerca de su lugar actual queda en la parte baja. Reubicarlo en una casa sobre losa, lo que significa abrir concreto, cambiar la línea y reparar, lo lleva a la parte alta.

**Reparación de estructura y sustrato: $0 a $900.**
Cero si la pared esta sana. Esta línea aparece después de la demolición, no antes, y por eso tu alcance debe decir por escrito que pasa si se encuentra daño.

**Cemento board: $250 a $700.**
El sustrato sobre el que va la impermeabilización. El cemento board no es una barrera de agua por si solo.

**Membrana impermeable: $350 a $1,100.**
La capa que decide si esta ducha dura.

![Membrana impermeable aplicada en la ducha antes del azulejo](/images/glenvar-waterproofing.jpeg "Esta es la línea que no vuelves a ver después del azulejo, y la primera que borra un presupuesto barato.")

**Construcción de la base: $450 a $1,400.**
Construida y con pendiente en sitio para drenar correctamente. Una base prefabricada cuesta menos y es una opción válida, pero fija tu tamaño y la altura del borde.

**Material de azulejo: $350 a $2,500.**
Porcelanato estandar abajo. Formato grande, mosaicos, piezas de borde y material importado arriba.

**Mano de obra de azulejo: $1,100 a $3,200.**
Depende más de la complejidad del patron que de los pies cuadrados.

**Lechada, borde y sellado: $180 a $600.**

**Accesorios: $220 a $1,200.**
Válvula, regadera, rociador de mano, barras de apoyo. Las barras necesitan refuerzo detras de la pared en el día dos, así que decide temprano aunque las instales después.

**Vidrio: $550 a $3,500.**
Un panel fijo es el extremo bajo. Un cerramiento sin marco con puerta es el alto.

**Permiso e inspecciones: $200 a $600.**
Una conversión toca plomería, así que necesita permiso en Miami-Dade incluso con HB 803. La explicación completa esta en [necesitas permiso para convertir una tina en ducha](/es/blog/tub-to-shower-permit-miami-dade).

**Limpieza final y prueba de agua: $150 a $400.**

## Por Que las Columnas No Se Suman Directo

Suma la columna baja y llegas cerca de $4,600, y por eso nuestro piso publicado es $4,500.

No sumes la columna alta. Llega a casi $20,000, y ningún proyecto real toca el máximo de todas las líneas a la vez. Alguien que reubica el desagüe a traves de una losa normalmente no esta comprando además mosaico importado y cerramiento sin marco. El extremo alto de una conversión real en Miami queda cerca de $12,000 porque las decisiones caras se concentran en dos o tres líneas, no en trece.

## Que Cubren de Verdad los Números Baratos

Una conversión de $311 no es una ducha. Tampoco lo es una de $1,491. Esas cifras vienen de sitios que promedian una mezcla amplia de trabajos, incluyendo tareas parciales. Normalmente describen una de estas:

- Sacar una tina sin ningún trabajo de reemplazo
- Instalar un inserto prefabricado en un nicho ya preparado
- Esmaltar o restaurar una tina existente
- Un precio solo de mano de obra donde el propietario compra todo el material
- Un corte de tina, que es quitar una sección de la pared de la tina para entrar más fácil

Ninguno incluye base, impermeabilización, azulejo, vidrio, permisos ni retiro de escombros.

## Los Factores que Más Mueven el Precio

**El desagüe es la variable más grande.** Dejarlo cerca de donde esta puede ser la diferencia entre el nivel estandar y el personalizado por si solo, porque la mayoría de las casas de Miami están sobre losa de concreto.

**El patron del azulejo cuesta más que el precio del azulejo.** Si el presupuesto esta ajustado, compra mejor azulejo y colocalo recto en lugar de azulejo barato en un patron complicado.

**El vidrio es la línea más fácil de ajustar.** Pasar de un cerramiento sin marco a un panel fijo puede ahorrar más de mil dolares y muchas veces funciona mejor en un baño pequeño.

**El daño escondido es real y no es una táctica de venta.** En casas anteriores a 1990 lo encontramos con suficiente frecuencia como para presupuestarlo.

## Preguntas Frecuentes

**¿Por que su mínimo es $4,500 si hay sitios que listan promedios de Miami bajo $2,300?**

Porque esos promedios no describen una ducha de azulejo terminada. Los sitios de costos juntan una mezcla amplia de trabajos bajo una sola etiqueta, incluyendo retiros de tina sin reemplazo, instalaciones de insertos prefabricados en nichos ya preparados, esmaltado de tinas, cortes de tina, y precios solo de mano de obra donde el propietario compra todo el material. Promedia todo eso y sale un número de pocos miles que no corresponde a ningún proyecto completo. Una conversión de azulejo terminada tiene que incluir demolición y retiro de escombros, ajuste de plomería por el cambio de tamaño del desagüe, cemento board, membrana impermeable, base con pendiente, azulejo y su mano de obra, lechada, accesorios, vidrio, permiso y prueba final de agua. Suma el piso realista de cada uno y llegas cerca de $4,600. Eso es aritmetica, no posicionamiento.

**¿Cuál es la forma más rápida de bajar el precio sin arrepentirse?**

Deja el desagüe donde esta y simplifica el vidrio. Esas dos decisiones mueven más dinero que cualquier elección de azulejo, y ninguna se siente como una renuncia en el uso diario. Reubicar el desagüe en una casa sobre losa significa abrir concreto, redirigir la línea, reparar y reconstruir el piso, lo que puede sumar más de mil dolares por si solo. Los cerramientos sin marco a la medida son la otra línea grande, y en un baño pequeño de Miami un solo panel fijo muchas veces funciona mejor porque deja el cuarto más abierto y te da menos herrajes que limpiar. Lo que no recortariamos es la impermeabilización, la construcción de la base ni el permiso. Esos son los tres puntos donde ahorrar ahora cuesta más después, y los tres quedan invisibles una vez puesto el azulejo.

**¿Existe un costo por pie cuadrado que pueda usar?**

No de forma útil, y ten cuidado con quien te cotice uno. Una conversión esta dominada por costos fijos que no crecen con el área. La demolición, el trabajo de desagüe, la construcción de la base, el permiso y el vidrio cuestan casi lo mismo en un nicho de 30 pies cuadrados que en uno de 40. Solo el material de azulejo y parte de la mano de obra siguen los pies cuadrados. Eso significa que una cifra por pie cuadrado sacada de una ducha grande personalizada va a sobrevalorar un nicho estandar, y una sacada de un trabajo simple va a subvalorar cualquier proyecto con banco, entrada sin borde o desagüe reubicado. Pide el desglose por partidas. Toma dos minutos leerlo y te dice algo que un solo multiplicador nunca puede decir.
