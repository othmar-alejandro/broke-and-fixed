# Finding the Thirsty Runner
Where the demand actually is for tub to shower in Miami-Dade, and how to get in front of it

---

## The short version

You asked me to find the people who are already thirsty instead of writing better ads for people who are not. Here is what the research says, and it is not what I expected.

**1. The buyer you have been picturing is the wrong one.**

The single best study on this is Gell et al., *Journal of Aging and Health*, 2020, working from the National Health and Aging Trends Study, 7,499 older adults tracked 2015 to 2017. The finding:

> "A single incident fall does not appear to lead to home environment changes to reduce fall risk."

One fall does not make anyone buy. What does:

| Situation | Got a bathroom modification within a year |
|---|---|
| Afraid of falling, has family or social contact | **63.4%** |
| Not afraid of falling | 44.0% |
| Afraid of falling, **socially isolated** | Odds ratio **0.5**, meaning *less* likely than average |

Read that last row again. Same fear, same house, same tub. The one with nobody in their life **buys less.**

**The trigger is not the fall. The trigger is the adult child.** The job gets bought when somebody who loves them starts pushing.

So the thirsty runner in your park is not the 68 year old. It is the 45 year old daughter in Kendall who spent Sunday at her mother's house and watched her hold the towel bar getting out of the tub, and has not stopped thinking about it since. She has money, she is on Instagram twenty times a day, she is easy to reach at 40 to 55, and **nobody in Miami is running an ad at her.** Every tub-to-shower ad in this country is pointed at the person in the tub.

**2. About a third of the people who fill out a home services form did zero research first.**

Modernize surveyed 12,300 homeowners: 53% spend one to five hours researching before submitting a lead, and roughly **30% spend zero hours.** Not one hour. Zero.

That is not a shopper. That is someone reacting to something that happened this week. Those are your thirsty runners and they are already in the market today. You cannot target them, but you can be the one who picks up. Which is why speed to lead keeps coming back as the answer.

**3. Half of everyone you are currently paying to reach cannot buy from you.**

Miami-Dade owner-occupancy rate is **52.2%** (Census QuickFacts, 2020 to 2024). Almost half the county rents. Every impression served to a renter is money on the floor, and broad geo targeting has no idea who is who.

**4. Meta cannot tell you who is thirsty. It has never been able to, and it is getting worse.**

- March 2018: Meta killed Partner Categories, which is when property and homeowner-financial data left the targeting menu.
- 2024: detailed targeting exclusions removed.
- **January 15, 2026: Meta removed another wave of detailed targeting options. Ad sets using them stopped delivering that day.** The direction is one way.

There is no "owns a 1974 house in Kendall and her mother just had a scare" checkbox. There never will be.

So the answer is not a better targeting setting. It is three other things, in this order.

## Number one is the whole opportunity

Some numbers that make the case:

- **31%** of homeowners who renovated a bathroom did it for the needs of a **current** household member. That is a live trigger. Another 47% "anticipate needs in 5+ years," which is not a trigger and never converts, because present bias guarantees it. (Houzz Bathroom Trends 2025)
- Homeowners making accessibility improvements hit a record **3.2 million households** in 2022 to 2023, up from 2.3 million in 2018 to 2019. A **39% jump.** (Harvard JCHS 2025)
- The share of bathroom renovations addressing special needs went **54% in 2021, 66% in 2023, 68% in 2025.** (Houzz)
- Owners 65+ put **88%** of their improvement dollars into professional work, versus 76% for under 35. They do not DIY. They hire.
- **25%** of bathroom renovations removed the tub entirely, and **82% of those** used the space to make the shower bigger. That is your exact job, and it is one in four bathroom remodels.
- Miami-Dade is **18.3%** age 65 and over, and **27.3%** age 45 to 64. Roughly 45% of the county is 45 or older.

And the marketing psychology skill you asked me to use says why the adult child converts and the parent does not:

> **Status-Quo Bias.** People prefer the current state of affairs. Change requires effort and feels risky.
> **Loss Aversion.** Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good.

The parent is living inside status quo bias. Nothing has broken yet. The daughter is living inside loss aversion, and what she is afraid of losing is not money. It is her mother's independence, and eventually her mother's house. That fear is worth roughly double any gain you could describe, and it is already there. You do not have to create it. You just have to name it accurately.

And the skill's own warning, which I am going to hold you to:

> "Use the emotional layer to understand the customer, not as the headline mechanism of the ad. The hook should name the problem accurately, not manufacture a feeling that wasn't already there."

So: no scare ads. No hospital photos. The honest version is strong enough.

## The price anchor nobody uses

The daughter is not comparing your $6,500 to another remodeling company's $8,000. She is comparing it, quietly and without saying it out loud, to assisted living.

That is a completely different denominator and it is the most powerful anchor available in this entire category. From the psychology skill:

> **Anchoring Effect.** The first number people see heavily influences subsequent judgments. Show the higher price first.
> **Rule of 100.** For prices over $100, absolute discounts seem larger. Quote dollars, never percent.

**Before you use this, get the real Miami number.** Look up the current Genworth Cost of Care figure for assisted living in the Miami metro. I did not verify it and I am not putting an unverified number in your ad. Once you have it, the math writes itself: a shower that costs less than two months of the alternative, and it is a one time cost, not a monthly one.

# HOOKS FOR THE ADULT CHILD

New audience, new angle. These are written for a 45 to 60 year old in Miami-Dade thinking about their parent's house.

**Policy line I am holding.** Meta rejects copy implying you know something personal about the viewer's health or ability. Everything below talks about **the bathroom and the house**, never about a person's condition. Keep it there in anything you write from these.

---

**A1. Callout**
> EN: "If you have thought about your parents' bathroom and not said anything yet, this is for you."
> ES: "Si ya pensó en el baño de sus papás y todavía no ha dicho nada, esto es para usted."
> **First frame:** an older bathroom, empty, shot from the doorway. No people. Hold two seconds before any text.

**A2. Problem agitate**
> EN: "You are going to have this conversation with your parents eventually. Here is the number, so you can have it now instead of after something happens."
> ES: "Esta conversación con sus papás va a llegar. Aquí está el número, para que la tenga ahora y no después de que pase algo."
> **First frame:** two coffee cups on a kitchen table, one chair empty.

**A3. Price anchor**
> EN: "Six thousand five hundred, one time. Compare that to what a month of the alternative costs in Miami."
> ES: "Seis mil quinientos, una sola vez. Compárelo con lo que cuesta un mes de la otra opción aquí en Miami."
> **First frame:** two numbers side by side on navy, one second, nothing else. `⚠️ verify the assisted living figure before this runs`

**A4. Specificity, the 15 inches**
> EN: "A standard tub wall is fifteen inches. That is the whole problem, and it is a six day fix."
> ES: "La pared de una bañera normal tiene quince pulgadas. Ese es todo el problema, y se arregla en seis días."
> **First frame:** a tape measure held against the tub wall, reading 15. No talking.

**A5. Negative hook**
> EN: "Do not buy this because a salesman scared you. Buy it because you did the math."
> ES: "No compre esto porque un vendedor lo asustó. Cómprelo porque hizo las cuentas."
> **First frame:** you, straight to camera, flat delivery.

**A6. Founder, and this one is yours to tell if it is true**
> EN: "The first one of these I ever did was for my own family. That is why we build the wall instead of covering it."
> ES: "La primera de estas que hice fue para mi propia familia. Por eso construimos la pared en vez de taparla."
> **First frame:** you, in a finished bathroom, hand on the tile.
> `⚠️ only run this if it is true. Do not fake it.`

**A7. Jobs to be done**
> EN: "Nobody wants a shower. They want their mother to stay in her own house."
> ES: "Nadie quiere una ducha. Quieren que su mamá se quede en su propia casa."
> **First frame:** a front door with a wreath on it. Then the bathroom.

**Headlines for this set** (27 characters or fewer)

| EN | ES |
|---|---|
| `Before, not after` | `Antes, no después` |
| `$6,500. One time.` | `$6,500. Una sola vez.` |
| `15 inches. 6 days.` | `15 pulgadas. 6 días.` |
| `She keeps her house` | `Ella se queda en su casa` |
| `No step, no lip` | `Sin escalón, sin borde` |

# THE PLAN

## Now, no new spend

**1. Split the ad set by who you are talking to, not by language.**

That was the real mistake in my first version. I had you splitting English and Spanish, which is a formatting distinction. The distinction that matters is **who makes the decision.**

```
LEADS_TOF_Estimator_US-MIA_202608
├── BROAD_adultchild_advantage_Lead     40-58, $25/day
│   └── A1, A2, A4, A7 in EN and ES
└── BROAD_owner_advantage_Lead          55-65+, $25/day
    └── the objection and comparison hooks from doc 04
```

Both languages live inside both ad sets. Yes, this is two ad sets, which is what you originally asked for. The difference is it is split on something that actually changes the message.

**2. Build the retargeting audience today, even though you will not use it for a month.**

This is the biggest hole in the plan and it costs nothing to fix. Site visitors are the **only** true intent signal Meta has. Anyone who lands on that page already had their trigger fire. And a kitchen and bath decision runs a **180 day sales cycle**, so a prospecting-only campaign is throwing away almost everyone who was interested but not ready.

Create the website custom audience now so it is accumulating. Turn the retargeting ad set on when you cross $100 a day.

**3. Answer in 60 seconds.**

HBR studied 1.25 million leads across 29 companies: contact within one hour was **7x** more likely to qualify, versus **60x** better than waiting 24 hours. Average firm took **42 hours.** And roughly 30% of your leads did zero research before submitting, meaning they are in a live reactive state right now.

You cannot buy your way to the thirsty runner. You can be the only one holding a cooler when they look up.

## Next, when you have $500 to spend on it

**Build the list from Miami-Dade property records.** This is the version of your property-intelligence idea that pays off immediately.

The filter, using Florida Department of Revenue NAL roll fields, which are **free** by email request to PTOTechnology@floridarevenue.com:

| Filter | Field | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Single family only | DOR land use code | exclude condo |
| Old bathroom | ACT_YR_BLT | 1960 to 1995 |
| Owner occupied | Homestead exemption, field 110 | must be present |
| Long tenure | last qualified sale | 10+ years ago |
| Equity, Florida specific | just value minus assessed value | wide gap. Save Our Homes caps assessed growth, so a big spread directly signals long tenure plus appreciation |
| Can afford it | just value | floor and ceiling per ZIP |

**Two things you need to know before you build this.**

First, **Meta does not accept street address as a match key.** Only email, phone, name, city, state, ZIP. So a raw county list matches badly. You have to append email and phone first. PropStream does the filter and the skip trace in one place, about $99 a month plus roughly $0.12 per lead.

Second, and this is the one that could kill it: **if Meta classifies your ads as Housing, you lose ZIP targeting, age targeting, all exclusions, lookalikes entirely, and your radius gets forced to a 15 mile floor.** Lookalike audiences are flatly unavailable in Housing. Special Ad Audiences, the old workaround, were discontinued October 2022 and are not coming back. Custom audiences survive but since January 2025 require a certification.

The weight of evidence says a remodeling service is not a housing ad. But it is your declaration in Ads Manager, Meta's classifier can override you, and the penalty is account level. **Check the category picker before you build anything on top of this.** That is why the $5 a day test in the campaign plan comes first.

**And an honest conclusion I did not expect.** For a small, precisely filtered list, **direct mail beats Meta on fidelity.** Meta loses most of your list to match rates. Mail loses none of it. Roughly $0.60 to $0.90 per household all in, versus a Meta custom audience that reaches maybe half the names you paid to find. If you build the list, mail it and run it on Meta. The mail is the offer, the Meta ad is the second and third touch.

## Later

Feed sold jobs back to Meta as offline conversions. Meta's published lift for CAPI for CRM is **19% lower cost per quality lead** and a **44% lift in lead to quality lead rate**. The threshold is 200+ leads a month and you will be at 12 to 25, so this is a next-year item. Keep the manual sheet in the meantime.

## What I could not verify

Being straight about this, because it matters:

- **No study exists on the time between trigger and purchase** for a bathroom project. I looked hard. The research-hours data is the closest proxy.
- **The "78% buy from the first responder" stat is fake.** It traces to a vendor with no primary study. Do not use it.
- **Nobody publishes who pays**, the parent or the adult child. Gifts and inheritance fund 5% of all renovations, which is the ceiling on any "someone else paid" theory. Best working assumption: the parent pays, the child decides. Market to both, bill one.
- **CDC's bathroom-specific injury breakdown is from 2008.** It is the only one that exists. 234,094 ED visits, 68% in or around the tub or shower, and the injury rate for 85+ is nine times that of young adults. Old data, still the best available.
- **Miami-Dade insurance premium figures** only exist on brokerage blogs with no methodology. I did not cite any. And honestly the hurricane angle belongs to your exterior repairs line, not this one. Insurance payouts fund only 5% of renovations.
- **The Meta Ad Library keyword search only matches ad link titles, not body copy**, so competitive hook research through the API is not workable. Do it by hand at facebook.com/ads/library. Screenshot the first frame of the ten longest running competitor ads and classify each one. Whatever type dominates is the one to test against.
