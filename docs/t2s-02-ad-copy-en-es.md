# Ad Copy and Creative Briefs
Tub to Shower | Broke & Fixed | English and Spanish

---

## Rules these were written against

From the vault's analysis of 43.9 million ads: primary text survival is bimodal. **Under 50 characters is the highest performing band. 250 to 500 characters is the second peak. The 50 to 250 band is the dead zone.** Every long ad below sits between 250 and 500. Every short ad sits under 50. Nothing in between.

Headlines are written to **27 characters or fewer** so they render whole on Feed, even though Meta allows 40.

Descriptions are 25 to 30 characters and frequently do not show on mobile at all. They are here for completeness, not for effort.

The Spanish is written, not translated. Miami Spanish, usted form. It does not track the English word for word and should not.

Compliance held throughout: no "contractor," no "licensed," no "contratista," no "licenciado." No em dashes. No exclamation points. "Fully insured" and "totalmente asegurados" only.

### Spanish

**Primary text**
```
Usted ha visto los anuncios de baños en un día.

```

**Headline:** `Quitamos la pared vieja`
**Description:** `Garantía de un año`

### Creative brief
Vertical video, 20 to 25 seconds, phone shot, you on camera at a job site.

- 0 to 3 sec: you holding a piece of the liner material, or standing at a wall that is already open. First words: "This is what a one day bathroom actually is." Text on screen inside half a second.
- 3 to 8 sec: point at the open wall. Show what was behind it. Rot, old mortar, whatever is real.
- 8 to 18 sec: the new backer going up, the waterproofing going on, tile starting.
- 18 to 25 sec: finished shower, then "Two questions on our site, get your range."

Captions burned in, both cuts. Assume mute. Do not polish it. The vault is explicit that owner-on-camera phone video beats agency b-roll almost every time in this vertical.

## Refresh cadence

New creative every 10 to 14 days. That is what keeps 14 day frequency under 2.0 and CPM down 15 to 25 percent.

Cheapest way to feed it: give your crew a one page shot list and $50 per job for usable footage. And the single highest value thing you can film is **the estimate conversation, not the finished job.**

Shot list for the crew:
1. The bathroom before anything is touched, shot from the doorway.
2. The tub coming out.
3. The wall opened up, showing what was behind it.
4. Waterproofing going on, close.
5. First tile going up.
6. Finished, shot from the exact same spot as shot 1.
7. Ten seconds of the homeowner reacting, if they are willing.
